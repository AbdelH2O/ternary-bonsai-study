# Deleted artifacts (2026-09-30)

166 large binary files were deleted to reclaim disk space before the replication work. None of them is tracked by git, so this record and [DELETED_ARTIFACTS.json](DELETED_ARTIFACTS.json) are the only trace of them. The JSON lists every file with its path, size, SHA-256 at deletion, the hash recorded when it was made (where one exists) and its regeneration command.

**No reported number depends on these files.** Every raw result (per-token NLL vectors, comparison JSONs, gates, freezes, logs and reports) was kept. What was lost is the ability to *re-run* certain phases without first regenerating their inputs.

## What was deleted

| Group | Files | Logical size | Hash status at deletion |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Early-pilot variants `variants/bonsai2-{mlp,state}-dNN.gguf` | 9 | 64.9 GB | No hash had ever been recorded; the SHA-256 in this record is the first |
| Calibration-only variants in `variants/dose_v3/` | 13 | 93.7 GB | All 13 matched `candidate_sha256` in their patch manifests |
| `fresh_state/captures/*.bin` state captures | 144 | 54.0 GB | All 144 matched both `fresh_state/capture_gate.json` and `fresh_state/ARTIFACTS.json` |
| **Total** | **166** | **212.5 GB** | Every hashed file was byte-identical to what its experiment used |

The logical size overstates the space freed, because the variants were reflinks sharing most blocks with the original model. Free space reported by `df` was 243.46 GB immediately before deletion and 344.89 GB after it (+101.4 GB). The earlier btrfs exclusive-usage estimate was about 61 GB. Free space on this disk was also changing independently: it rose by roughly 70 GB between an earlier check and the pre-deletion measurement. So the share of the +101.4 GB due to this deletion is uncertain.

## What was kept

- The original checkpoint `models/bonsai2-gguf/27B/Ternary-Bonsai-2-27B-PQ2_0.gguf`. Its hash was verified after deletion (`3907dc16…62ec1`).
- The six frozen variants used by the held-out, fresh-state, controlled-state, margin and validation work: `alpha-d200-c1000-s2026092{9,30,31}` and `mlp-d200-c200-s2026092{9,30,31}`. All six were re-verified against their manifests after deletion.
- Every variant patch manifest (`*.json`), the pilot smoke logs and `variants/smoke.jsonl`.
- Everything in `fresh_state/captures/` except the `.bin` files: the per-capture `.json` raw scores, `.meta.json` hash records and `.log` files. Also all `fresh_state` gates, comparisons, the freeze and the report.
- All other capture sets: `remote_state_gate`, `remote_components`, `validation_gates`, `controlled_state` and `memory_gate/rebased`.

## Consequences for existing records

- `fresh_state/ARTIFACTS.json` and `fresh_state/capture_gate.json` still list the 144 deleted `.bin` files. Integrity checks over those inventories will report them missing; this document explains why. The inventories were deliberately not edited.
- **`memory_gate` inputs:** 24 of the deleted captures, the original-arm 1K and 12K recurrent states for all 12 spans, are its inputs (`short_state_path` / `long_state_path` in `memory_gate/MEMORY_GATE_FREEZE_V2.json`). Its completed results and report are unaffected. Re-running `memory_gate/gate_v2.py` now fails unless those 24 files are regenerated byte-identically, because it compares source digests with its saved metadata. The 12 position-rebased copies in `memory_gate/rebased/` remain.
- The `fresh_state` `positive` and `treatment` phases also hash-check the captures before running, so they can't be re-run until the captures are restored.

### `fresh_state` captures (host CUDA required)

The 144 files follow one pattern: 6 books × positions {30000, 50000} × histories {1024, 12288} × arms {original, alpha, mlp} × {full, recurrent}. For example: `fresh_state/captures/emma-p30000-h1024-original-full.bin`. Per-file hashes are in [DELETED_ARTIFACTS.json](DELETED_ARTIFACTS.json) and in `fresh_state/capture_gate.json`.

This procedure has **not** been run:

1. Back up `fresh_state/capture_gate.json` and the `fresh_state/captures/*.meta.json` files. The capture phase refuses to proceed while a `.meta.json` points at a missing `.bin`, so move aside the metadata for the captures you want back.
2. Run `python3 analysis/bonsai2/fresh_state/run.py capture` with host-device CUDA. It re-checks the pinned program, model, library, document and token-ID hashes first. The two variant arms need `alpha-d200-c1000-s20260929` and `mlp-d200-c200-s20260929`, which were kept.
3. Compare each regenerated `.bin` hash with this record.
   - **If they match,** restore the backed-up `capture_gate.json` and the downstream phases (`positive`, `treatment`, `memory_gate/gate_v2.py`) will accept the files.
   - **If they don't,** nobody has tested whether capture bytes are reproducible across runs. The capture gate's own NLL roundtrip checks can still show the new states are functionally equivalent, but using them in downstream phases would need a versioned technical amendment rather than silently replacing the recorded hashes.

## Deletion procedure used

1. Listed the 166 target files.
2. Ran `sha256sum` on every file and compared each hash with every existing record: the patch manifests, `capture_gate.json` and `ARTIFACTS.json`. There were no mismatches.
3. Wrote [DELETED_ARTIFACTS.json](DELETED_ARTIFACTS.json).
4. Deleted the files and confirmed none remained.
5. Re-hashed the original model and the six kept frozen variants; all matched.
